Title: Informing risk stratification and vaccine policy via a predictive mathematical model of SARS-COV-2 infection
Link: https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.12.26365315v1?rss=1"
Summary: SARS-CoV-2 human challenge studies enabled unprecedented high frequency temporal measurements of immune determinants of viral control. We integrated prior knowledge of SARS-CoV-2 replication, innate, cytotoxic T cell and antibody responses into a dynamic model which captured viral-load kinetics in seronegative volunteers challenged with wildtype virus. The model predicted measured innate and adaptive immune dynamics. Perturbation analyses indicated that innate immunity limits peak viral load, whereas antibodies principally mediate clearance while cytotoxic T cells become critical in antibody deficiency. Pre-existing antibodies suppressed both viral replication and subsequent immune activation- a prediction validated in a second challenge study of seropositive participants infected with the SARS-CoV-2 Delta variant. The model explains how specific immunodeficiencies may increase susceptibility to severe or persistent infection, supporting clinical risk stratification. It suggests that T cell-targeted vaccination strategies may provide limited protection without effective neutralising antibodies, and that attenuated boosting after low-dose exposure requires repeated vaccination to reduce breakthrough infections, informing public health policy.

One Sentence SummaryA mathematical model of SARS-COV-2 infection predicts innate and adaptive immunity, and informs risk stratification and vaccine policy.


Title: Vaccine attitudes and perceptions in a post-COVID Era: what lessons can be learned in Tunisia?
Link: https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.02.26364577v1?rss=1"
Summary: IntroductionAlthough the COVID-19 pandemic clearly showed the potential consequences of a world without vaccinations, vaccine hesitancy is likely to be more pronounced in the post-COVID-19 era. The introduction of human papillomavirus (HPV) vaccines in this context is likely to raise particular concerns. In Tunisia, decision-makers have prioritized the introduction of HPV vaccine into the immunization schedule. This study assessed individual barriers and facilitators to HPV vaccine acceptability in Tunisia, with the aim of guiding an effective communication strategy for HPV vaccination campaigns in Tunisia and neighboring countries in a post-pandemic context.

MethodsUsing a qualitative design, we conducted 14 focus groups (FG) with healthcare professionals (HCP) (N=32) and community representatives (CR) (N=89) in five regions of the country. The discussion followed a semi-structured guide designed on the basis of vaccine hesitancy determinants matrix. Transcripts were deductively analyzed according to the "social processes" and "thinking and feeling" of the behavioural and social drivers (BeSD) Framework. Inductive approach was used to analyze participants suggestions for promoting HPV vaccination.

ResultsThe FG analysis demonstrated that the historically strong trust in vaccination in Tunisia has weakened after the COVID-19 pandemic. Mistrust of health system and experts, the pandemic management and vaccine mandates measures had negative influence on the vaccine attitudes in a post pandemic Tunisian context. Persistent concerns about COVID-19 vaccines safety were frequently intertwined with widespread distrust of pharmaceutical companies and their profit motives, perceived as imposing inequitable access and lower-quality vaccines on countries in the Global South. These concerns fueled confusion about vaccines side effects. The weakening of immunity was recurring, as well as memory issues, impacts on fertility, and sudden deaths. Regarding information, our research revealed that although digital platforms were perceived as widely used, a significant part of the population interviewed considered public TV channel, as the most reliable source of health information.

ConclusionsEmphasizing how the pandemic has shaped individual and collective perceptions and attitudes toward vaccination, our findings underline the importance of leveraging lessons learned from the pandemic to develop a context tailored and coherent communication strategy for successful HPV vaccine implementation.


Title: Antiviral Antibody Fc Dysfunction Is Linked to Increased IgG1 Fucosylation in Long COVID
Link: https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.09.28.26364044v1?rss=1"
Summary: BackgroundPersistent SARS-CoV-2 antigen is a leading hypothesis for post-acute sequelae of SARS-CoV-2 infection (PASC), commonly known as Long COVID. Impaired Fc-mediated antiviral antibody function could contribute to inefficient clearance of infected cells and viral antigen despite preserved antibody levels. Therefore, we tested whether PASC is associated with altered Fc{gamma} receptor-mediated antibody function and differences in IgG1 Fc fucosylation, a major regulator of Fc effector function.

MethodsSerum samples from RECOVER-Adult participants with prior SARS-CoV-2 infection (99 PASC, 139 non-PASC) were analyzed for anti-SARS-CoV-2 spike receptor-binding domain (RBD) IgG and subclasses, Fc{gamma}RIIIa- and Fc{gamma}RIIa-mediated reporter activity as surrogate measures of antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) and antibody-dependent cellular phagocytosis (ADCP), anti-RBD IgG1 Fc glycosylation, and circulating fucose-modifying enzymes.

ResultsDespite higher anti-RBD IgG and IgG1 abundance, PASC sera showed lower ADCC- and ADCP-associated Fc{gamma}R reporter activity, including after normalization for antibody abundance. Anti-RBD IgG1 showed greater fucosylation in PASC samples (30 PASC, 34 non-PASC). Serum FUCA2 was lower in PASC samples (24 PASC, 34 non-PASC), whereas FUCA1 and soluble CD16a levels did not differ between the two groups.

ConclusionPASC is associated with impaired antiviral IgG Fc function, increased IgG1 Fc fucosylation, and reduced circulating FUCA2, identifying a glycoimmune axis that may contribute to impaired Fc-dependent antigen clearance and represents a potentially targetable pathway in Long COVID.
